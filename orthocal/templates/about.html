About

Orthocal.info is an Eastern Orthodox calendar service providing commemorations, fasting, scripture readings and other information for each day of the liturgical year.

Two liturgical traditions are supported. OCA reflects the practice of the Orthodox Church in America, whose calendar this project's Slavic-tradition data was compiled from; it also serves the Russian Orthodox Church Outside of Russia (ROCOR) well. GOA reflects the practice of the Greek Orthodox Archdiocese, and also suits the Antiochian Archdiocese: the two keep the same lectionary almost everywhere, differing on roughly four days a year. This option is still in beta while its data continues to be verified. Either tradition can be viewed on the (New) Gregorian or (Old) Julian calendar.

Fasting indications for the OCA tradition follow the OCA's Fasting & Fast-Free Seasons of the Church. The GOA tradition observes the same fasting seasons but with a few differences, the most notable being a more lenient first phase of the Nativity Fast and a handful of additional wine-and-oil allowances during Great Lent.

The primary purpose of this service is to provide convenient access to daily devotional material for lay persons and not to be an authoritative or comprehensive guide to the feasts and fasts of the Church. The service supports this goal by providing tools for mobile developers and parish webmasters, an Alexa skill, multiple types of feeds, and a web interface.

Technology & Data

The Source Code is freely available. The original version of this site was written in a combination of Go and Node.js, but has been rewritten in Python and Django since the author finds that much easier to maintain. The site is deployed on Google Cloud Run and Firebase. The algorithm and calendar data for the Slavic tradition are based on the algorithm and data developed by Paul Kachur for his orthodox_calendar project. The lives of the saints are taken by permission from John Brady's Abbamoses.com. Calendar data for the Greek tradition was compiled from the Antiochian Archdiocese's website (antiochian.org) and cross-checked against the Greek Orthodox Archdiocese of America (goarch.org).

Scriptures

Because this is open-source and free, the service must use a translation of the Bible that is either public domain or has agreeable license terms. The default translation for the daily readings is LXX2012 + WEB, a modern-English pairing with an Old Testament translated directly from the Septuagint. The Eastern Orthodox Bible (EOB) project began as a revision of the WEB. Both LXX2012 and WEB come from Michael Paul Johnson's public-domain modernization work, published through eBible.org. LXX2012 updates Sir Lancelot Brenton's 1851 translation of the Septuagint, so it follows the same versification and Old Testament content—including the books outside the Protestant canon—that the lectionary itself is based on. The WEB (World English Bible) is an update of the 1901 American Standard Version, using the Byzantine Majority Text for the New Testament. It is a well-respected, easy-to-read translation without the interpretive biases of translations like the NIV. Both are public domain.

The King James Version is also available as an alternative on the readings page, and remains the default for the API and RSS feeds. While not ideal, many clergy consider this to be one of the best options for Orthodox. Because the rubrics use Septuagint versification, a handful of the Old Testament readings may be incorrect.

The Douay-Rheims (Challoner, American Edition of 1899) is also available as an alternative on the readings page. It is a translation from the Latin Vulgate rather than the Greek Septuagint, so its Old Testament content and versification occasionally differ from the lectionary's own—most notably, it keeps Susanna, Bel and the Dragon, and the Song of the Three Youths as chapters within Daniel rather than as separate books, so any reading that cites those books by name will not be available in this translation. Sourced, like LXX2012 and WEB, from eBible.org; it is public domain.

Some of the composite readings are taken from the translations of the Archimandrite Ephrem (Lash). His translations can be accessed at Anastasis.
